# mef3io 2.1.0

*Draft — not yet released.*

A minor release that makes writing many channels much faster. Nothing that
worked in 2.0 changes.

## What's new

- **Write many channels in one call.** `Writer.write_int32_many(channels,
  data, ufact, start_uutc, fs)` (integer counts) and `Writer.write_many(...)`
  (float data, NaN gaps, precision decided per channel) write the same stretch
  of time for all channels at once. The files are the same as a loop of
  `write_int32` / `write` calls would produce (a fresh segment differs only
  in its random UUIDs). The channels are encoded and written in parallel on
  the `Writer`'s `n_threads`, and the disk is waited for once per call
  instead of once per channel. Also in C++ (`SessionWriter::write_int32_many`,
  `write_float_many`), the C ABI (`mef3io_writer_write_int32_many`) and
  MATLAB (`Writer.writeInt32Many`, integer counts only).
  *Speed:* on synthetic data (64 channels of 10 minutes at 2048 Hz, appended
  in 4 rounds, 12 threads, one SSD, one 12-core machine) a loop of
  `write_int32` took 5.2 s and `write_int32_many` 0.76 s. Your gain depends on
  your disk and channel count; it is largest with many channels and a disk
  where waiting for a sync is slow. Numbers and the setup are in the
  [benchmarks](https://bnelair.github.io/mef3io/benchmarks/).
- **`Writer(flush="close")` (opt-in).** No wait for the disk during writes;
  `close()` or `flush()` does one combined wait. **If the power is cut while
  writing, the whole session can become unreadable, and `recover_session`
  cannot be relied on to fix it.** Use it only for output you rebuild after
  any failure. The default, `flush="each"`, is unchanged. It is refused
  together with `wait_for_disk=True` and on a session that already existed
  when the `Writer` opened, so earlier recordings are never put at risk.
  Batching with `write_int32_many` already removes most of the waiting; in the
  same synthetic test `flush="close"` saved only about 10 % more on top of it.
  See [When is my data on disk?](https://bnelair.github.io/mef3io/long_recordings/#when-is-my-data-on-disk).

## Failures in a multi-channel write

Every refusal (a duplicate channel, a different rate or conversion factor
than what is stored, data starting before the stored end) is decided before
any file is touched. A failure before the metadata files are published rolls
every channel back. A failure while they are being published raises
`PartialWriteError`, whose `written_channels` and `incomplete_channels` say
what was written. If a roll-back itself fails, `RollbackIncompleteError` names
the channels whose files may be torn, never a silent "nothing written": run
`validate` and `recover` on them.

## Changed

- Writing a fresh segment copies the encoded blocks once less.

## Upgrading from 2.0

Nothing is required: no signature, default or file format changed, and the
new calls and option are additive. To get the speed-up, replace a loop of
`write_int32` / `write` over channels with one `write_int32_many` /
`write_many` call, and write long runs in chunks of minutes (each call holds
its channels' data and encoded blocks in memory).
